Before you make an offer

  1. Land classification. Ask the ayuntamiento for a certificado urbanístico. On suelo rústico / no urbanizable, new houses are usually banned or need huge minimum plots. Renovating an existing, legally registered building is the realistic path.
  2. Is the house legal? Check it appears in the Catastro and the Registro de la Propiedad (nota simple, ~€10). Unregistered ruins may not be rebuildable.
  3. Protected area rules. Inside a Parque Natural or Red Natura 2000 zone, the PORN/PRUG plan decides what you may build, cut or fence. Read it for the exact parcel.
  4. Fire history. Under the Ley de Montes (Ley 43/2003, art. 50) burned forest land cannot change use for 30 years. Check past fires on EFFIS.
  5. Monte público or vecinal? Many forests are montes de utilidad pública or montes vecinales en mano común (Galicia). You can buy next to them, not in them.
  6. Water. Spring or well rights (concesión from the Confederación Hidrográfica) matter more than mains water in remote places.
  7. Access and services. A right of way (servidumbre de paso) must be registered. Check electricity connection cost and mobile coverage on site.

Living in a dense forest safely

  • Defensible space. Most regions require a 25–50 m cleared strip (franja de protección) around buildings in forest-urban interface. Galicia enforces 50 m under Ley 3/2007.
  • Species matter. Beech, oak and chestnut burn far less readily than pine or eucalyptus. Atlantic broadleaf forests (Navarra, Asturias, Cantabria, País Vasco) carry the lowest risk.
  • Eucalyptus and pine plantations often appear as "forest" in ads. They are high fire risk and low habitat value. Ask what the trees are.
  • Insurance. Get a fire insurance quote before buying. Refusal or a very high premium tells you something.
  • Winter. North-facing slopes in Cantabrian valleys get little sun from November to February. Visit in winter.

Where to search

  • Idealista: filter "Terrenos" or "Casas de pueblo/rústicas", then search the municipality names from the Forests tab.
  • Fotocasa: section "Fincas rústicas" by province.
  • Milanuncios: private sellers, keyword "bosque" or "monte".
  • Cocampo: specialist in fincas forestales and rústicas.
  • Aldeas Abandonadas: villages, ruins and pallazas in the north-west.
  • AgroAnuncios: rural plots, many with robledal or castaños.
  • Galicia: the Banco de Terras lists public and abandoned plots.

Map tools

  • SIGPAC viewer: land use per parcel (FO = forestal).
  • Mapa Forestal de España: species and canopy cover (fracción de cabida cubierta).
  • Iberpix (IGN): aerial photos, including historical ones to see how the forest has changed.
  • Protected areas (ENP / Natura 2000) boundaries.
